# Delivery notes

## What I would build next

I would finish the one known release blocker: create a hash-allowlisted packet,
run the final candidate in a provably brand-new non-inheriting red-team task,
record the isolation manifest and red-team task telemetry, then reconcile and
revalidate the result. After that, I would add retained process-RSS sampling to
complement the current Python-allocation benchmark and broaden the hostile-format
fixture set.

## What surprised me

The first shadow report passed mechanical validation while mapping transaction
answers to the wrong headings, reversing variance wording and retaining stale
vision limitations. A structurally different synthetic room and human review
caught defects that the primary-room golden path did not. The exercise reinforced
that a clean schema and passing arithmetic do not by themselves establish a
decision-useful conclusion.

## What I am least confident about

Issue recall on highly unstructured, image-heavy or genuinely novel confidential
rooms remains less certain than registration, extraction and citation integrity.
Optional OCR was absent in the acceptance environment. Irish legal and tax output
is commercial diligence, not a formal professional opinion. Most importantly,
the historical challenge and resolution logs do not prove the independent
red-team context required for release.

## What I deliberately prioritised

I prioritised evidence provenance, native citations, transparent recomputation,
real human intake stops, resumable failure state and honest logging over breadth
of integrations or polished automation. I kept Python deterministic and local,
left unavailable model IDs/tokens/costs null with reasons, and preserved negative
acceptance evidence instead of turning a candidate validation into a release
claim.

## What I would change with a larger budget

I would use an actually exposed economical model for bulk classification and a
separately verifiable frontier context for red team, recording actual provider
usage only when the harness exposes it. I would add diverse sealed rooms,
independent Irish legal/tax specialist review, multi-OS and offline clone
benchmarks, fuzzed parsers, long-duration tests and process-level memory metrics.

## Known limitations

The checked-in output is a reconciled synthetic candidate whose validation says
`release_ready: false`; no packet allowlist, sealed-packet manifest or isolation
manifest exists. The clean-clone proof is Windows-only, so macOS/Linux commands
are documented but not locally executed. Offline installation, production archive
limits, confidential-run retention policy and quantitative recall/false-positive
thresholds remain evaluator decisions. The synthetic room is useful evidence of
behaviour, not a guarantee of completeness on every real data room.
